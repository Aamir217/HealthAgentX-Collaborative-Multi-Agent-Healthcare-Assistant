# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by insulin
resistance and relative insulin deficiency, leading to hyperglycemia.

## Diagnostic Criteria
Fasting plasma glucose >= 126 mg/dL, HbA1c >= 6.5%, or a random glucose >= 200
mg/dL with classic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss).
Fasting glucose 100-125 mg/dL or HbA1c 5.7-6.4% indicates prediabetes.

## Clinical Findings
Fatigue, increased thirst, frequent urination, blurred vision, slow-healing
wounds, and recurrent infections are common presenting symptoms. Many
patients are asymptomatic and identified on routine lab screening.

## Report Insights
Elevated fasting glucose or HbA1c on a lab panel should prompt evaluation for
diabetes. Elevated triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol frequently
co-occur with insulin resistance (metabolic syndrome).

## Management
First-line pharmacologic therapy is typically metformin, alongside lifestyle
modification (diet, exercise, weight loss). Metformin should be held before
and after administration of iodinated contrast dye in patients with renal
impairment due to risk of lactic acidosis. Regular monitoring of renal
function, HbA1c, and screening for retinopathy/neuropathy/nephropathy is
recommended.

## Risk Indicators
Uncontrolled diabetes significantly increases cardiovascular risk. Very high
glucose (>300 mg/dL) with symptoms of dehydration, confusion, or rapid
breathing may indicate diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic
state, both medical emergencies.
