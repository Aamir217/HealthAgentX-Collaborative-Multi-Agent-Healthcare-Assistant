# Chest Pain Triage

Chest pain has a broad differential ranging from benign musculoskeletal
strain to immediately life-threatening cardiac or pulmonary conditions.

## Red Flags (Emergent)
- Crushing, pressure-like chest pain radiating to the arm, jaw, or back
- Chest pain with diaphoresis, nausea, or shortness of breath
- Chest pain with syncope or near-syncope
- Sudden, tearing chest/back pain (consider aortic dissection)
- Chest pain with hemoptysis or unilateral leg swelling (consider pulmonary
  embolism)

Any of the above findings warrant emergent evaluation (e.g. ECG, troponin,
emergency department referral) rather than routine follow-up.

## Cardiac Causes
Acute coronary syndrome, stable/unstable angina, pericarditis, and
myocarditis. Risk factors include hypertension, diabetes, smoking,
hyperlipidemia, and family history of early cardiovascular disease.

## Non-Cardiac Causes
Musculoskeletal strain, costochondritis, gastroesophageal reflux disease,
anxiety/panic attacks, and pneumonia can all present with chest discomfort
and should be considered once emergent causes are excluded.

## Suggested Next Steps
For any chest pain with red-flag features, recommend urgent in-person
evaluation with ECG and cardiac biomarkers rather than relying on remote
assessment alone.
