# Common Drug Interactions

## Warfarin + NSAIDs (e.g. Ibuprofen) or Aspirin
Combining anticoagulants like warfarin with NSAIDs or antiplatelet agents
significantly increases the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding and other
major hemorrhage. Concomitant use should be avoided or closely monitored
with INR checks.

## ACE Inhibitors (e.g. Lisinopril) + Potassium Supplements
ACE inhibitors reduce aldosterone-mediated potassium excretion. Combined
with potassium supplements or potassium-sparing diuretics, this can cause
dangerous hyperkalemia, which may present with muscle weakness or cardiac
arrhythmia.

## Statins (e.g. Simvastatin) + Grapefruit
Grapefruit juice inhibits CYP3A4 metabolism of many statins, raising blood
levels and increasing the risk of myopathy and, rarely, rhabdomyolysis.
Patients on simvastatin or atorvastatin should limit grapefruit intake.

## Metformin + Iodinated Contrast Dye
In patients with renal impairment, iodinated contrast media can reduce renal
clearance of metformin, increasing the risk of lactic acidosis. Metformin is
typically withheld around the time of contrast administration.

## PDE5 Inhibitors (e.g. Sildenafil) + Nitrates
Combining PDE5 inhibitors with nitroglycerin or other nitrates can cause
severe, life-threatening hypotension. This combination is contraindicated.

## MAOIs + SSRIs
Combining monoamine oxidase inhibitors with SSRIs risks serotonin syndrome,
a potentially life-threatening condition presenting with agitation,
hyperthermia, and autonomic instability.
