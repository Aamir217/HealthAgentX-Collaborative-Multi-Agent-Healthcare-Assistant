# Respiratory Infections (Upper and Lower)

## Common Cold / Upper Respiratory Infection
Presents with rhinorrhea, sore throat, mild cough, and low-grade or absent
fever, typically self-limited over 7-10 days. Supportive care (rest,
fluids, analgesics) is the mainstay of treatment.

## Influenza
Sudden onset of fever, myalgia, headache, fatigue, and dry cough. Antiviral
treatment (e.g. oseltamivir) is most effective when started within 48 hours
of symptom onset, particularly in high-risk patients.

## Community-Acquired Pneumonia
Fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and shortness of breath.
Physical exam may reveal crackles or decreased breath sounds. Elevated
white blood cell count (WBC) and hypoxia (low SpO2) are supportive findings.
Severe presentations (respiratory rate > 30, hypotension, SpO2 < 90%, or
confusion) suggest need for hospitalization.

## Red Flags
Shortness of breath at rest, SpO2 below 92%, high fever unresponsive to
antipyretics, or confusion in the setting of respiratory symptoms warrant
urgent evaluation.
