# Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

Hypertension is defined as a sustained systolic blood pressure of 130 mmHg or
higher, or a diastolic blood pressure of 80 mmHg or higher, measured on
multiple occasions. It is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease,
stroke, and chronic kidney disease.

## Clinical Findings
Most patients are asymptomatic ("silent killer"), but severe or rapidly
rising blood pressure can present with headache, dizziness, blurred vision,
chest pain, shortness of breath, or epistaxis. Hypertensive urgency is a
systolic reading above 180 mmHg or diastolic above 120 mmHg without acute
end-organ damage; hypertensive emergency is the same threshold WITH signs of
end-organ damage (e.g. chest pain, altered mental status, vision loss) and
requires immediate treatment.

## Risk Factors
Obesity, high sodium intake, sedentary lifestyle, chronic stress, family
history, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease all increase risk.

## Management
First-line pharmacologic options include ACE inhibitors (e.g. lisinopril),
ARBs, calcium channel blockers, and thiazide diuretics. ACE inhibitors
should be used cautiously with potassium supplements or potassium-sparing
diuretics due to hyperkalemia risk. Lifestyle modification (sodium
restriction, weight loss, exercise) is recommended alongside medication.

## Red Flags
Blood pressure readings above 180/120 mmHg combined with chest pain, severe
headache, shortness of breath, or neurological symptoms constitute a
hypertensive emergency and require urgent evaluation.
